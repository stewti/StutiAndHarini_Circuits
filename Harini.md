This task challenged us to think beyond conventional components and approach a basic electrical circuit in a more creative and interactive way. Given only a cell, LED, and wires, our goal was not just to complete the circuit but to make the interaction meaningful. Instead of using a regular switch, we experimented with everyday objects and chose a fork as a conductor to complete the circuit.
The final idea took the form of a cupcake, with the entire circuit hidden behind a cut-out. Copper tape was placed strategically so that when the fork touched it, the circuit was completed and the cherry on top of the cupcake glowed. This transformed a simple electrical concept into a playful, user-driven interaction.
The process involved exploring many different ideas before arriving at this one. One of the main challenges we faced was figuring out how to make the circuit interactive using only the limited tools provided. We also had to ensure proper contact between the fork and the copper tape so the LED would glow consistently, which required multiple trials and adjustments.
Through this exercise, we learned that constraints can actually push creativity rather than limit it. We understood how everyday objects can function as electrical components and how design thinking plays a role even in technical tasks. More importantly, we realized that interaction doesn’t always require complex technology, it can be achieved through simple, thoughtful design decisions. This task helped us combine function, creativity, and storytelling into one simple yet interactive outcome.
